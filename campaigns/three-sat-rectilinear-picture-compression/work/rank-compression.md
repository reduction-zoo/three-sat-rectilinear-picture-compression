# Rank compression preserves rectangle covers

Let P be a bounded regular closed orthogonal polygonal region, possibly with holes or several components, specified by finitely many rational vertices. Let X and Y be the sorted distinct coordinates of its vertical and horizontal boundary lines, with p and q elements. Form a (q−1)×(p−1) binary matrix M: an entry is one exactly when the open cell between its two consecutive X and Y coordinates lies in P. Boundary coordinates ensure each such open cell is wholly inside or wholly outside. P is the union of the closures of its inside cells.

**Claim.** P and M have equal minimum numbers of axis-aligned rectangles in an exact cover. Every matrix cover lifts in polynomial time to a polygon cover of the same size. Thus a polynomial-size rational polygon construction suffices for a polynomial-size explicit matrix construction; a polynomial bound on geometric area is unnecessary.

Choose strictly increasing continuous piecewise-linear bijections f and g mapping X[i] to i and Y[j] to j, extending linearly outside their extremes. The product map H(x,y)=(f(x),g(y)) is a homeomorphism. It maps every axis-aligned rectangle [a,b]×[c,d] to [f(a),f(b)]×[g(c),g(d)] and preserves containments and unions. This statement includes rectangle boundaries not on polygon coordinates. H(P) is exactly the union of closed unit cells represented by M.

For a positive-area rectangle contained in H(P), expand its four sides to the integer boundaries of all open unit cells it intersects. The touched cell rows and columns form consecutive intervals, and their Cartesian product consists only of one cells: each touched cell has a positive-area intersection with the rectangle and therefore cannot be an outside cell. The expanded rectangle is consequently still contained in H(P). Expanding every rectangle of a cover yields a discrete cover with no more rectangles. Degenerate rectangles may be discarded: the finite union of the positive-area closed rectangles already covers the dense interior and hence all of the regular closed region. Conversely, a discrete rectangle with inclusive cell indices (r0,r1,c0,c1) lifts to [X[c0],X[c1+1]]×[Y[r0],Y[r1+1]]. These lifted rectangles cover P exactly and preserve count. The empty region is immediate.

If there are n input vertices, p,q≤n, so M has at most (n−1)² entries. Sorting and comparisons use polynomial bit complexity. Cell membership can be found by exact point-in-polygon tests at rational midpoints. Lifting uses only coordinate lookups; G needs no geometric optimization. The same proof applies when P is given as a union of t rectangles, with p,q≤2t. The accompanying implementation uses that representation.

This corrects the inference made in round 004. Its 2^b×1 example refutes naive unit rasterization, but compresses to a single one cell and is no obstruction to the reduction. A complete published-gadget reconstruction still needs a deterministic finite layout and decoder.
