# Conditional composition lemma for rectangle-cover modules

This lemma states explicit hypotheses; the campaign has not established them for an arbitrary source layout.

Let a binary picture be the union of modules whose required cells are disjoint. An interface joins one output port of a predecessor to one input port of a successor. Assume:

1. Every maximal legal rectangle either lies in one module, or is one designated interface rectangle. Each interface rectangle meets exactly its two modules, and restricts there to the predecessor's designated output rectangle and the successor's supplied input cells.
2. All designated interface rectangles are legal. Restricting an internal cover rectangle and extending it within its module is legal.
3. For every input set I and required output set O, the module has an independently established lower bound L(I,O) on the number of rectangles needed to cover its required cells after input cells I are supplied, with its output rectangles O included. A matching upper cover is available for the states used in the construction.

Extend every rectangle of any cover to a maximal rectangle, removing duplicates. For each selected interface rectangle set its signal to one and count it at its predecessor. Count every other rectangle in its containing module. At a module, the incoming interface rectangles supply exactly the stipulated input cells for free; its counted rectangles cover every remaining required cell and include each selected output. Therefore its count is at least L(I,O). Summing gives a lower bound on the original cover size because every rectangle was assigned exactly once. Conversely, compatible local covers glue to a global cover: extend selected outputs through their interfaces, omit supplied incoming rectangles from the successor's count, and take the union. Its size is at most the sum of local counts. These transformations use rectangle extension and membership tests, hence polynomial time for explicit finite pictures.

For routing modules with L(I,O)=B+|O\I| (with the appropriate fixed channel permutation), this charges one rectangle whenever a channel becomes present without its predecessor. If sources charge one when any of their output beams is selected, and an edge module charges one unless at least one endpoint input is present, a vertex cover can be recovered by selecting active source vertices, the source vertex belonging to every route with a newly created signal, and one endpoint of every remaining uncovered edge. Deduplication only reduces size. Its size is at most the cover's excess over the sum of baselines. This argument also requires a source-independent routing layout in which every edge channel has a known originating vertex and no unintended fanout occurs.

The two trimmed swap modules in round 035 satisfy the first hypothesis by direct enumeration of **all** maximal rectangles: exactly `(1,49,55,55)` and `(26,74,44,44)` cross the row-37 cut. A geometric alternative is to inspect their adjacent boundary rows: their common columns are precisely 44 and 55, each isolated, and the column runs stop inside the two modules. The single-module baseline is 44; the two-module baseline is 88. All 16 local and 16 composed required-output states were checked, and all 16 exact maximal-output states were also checked. These finite checks do not provide arbitrary-graph coordinates, source/edge modules meeting the hypotheses, or a complete F/G.
